Assistant

A spoiler-free viewing companion, built on Gemini and Google Cloud.

Pause any film and ask. It answers only from what you have already seen: it never names a character before the film does, never hints at a twist and never says an actor’s name.

The problem

Viewers pause with questions. Every answer out there is a spoiler risk.

“Who is she?”A face you can’t place
“What is that?”An object that seems to matter
“What did I miss?”You stepped away for ten minutes
“What did he just say?”A line lost to the soundtrack

A general chatbot knows the whole film

From reviews, wikis and its training data. It cannot tell what you have seen, so it answers with everything it knows.

Who is the old man? (minute 12)
He’s the hero’s mentor, who turns out to be … — the reveal from minute 80.

Today viewers rewind, search the web and risk spoilers, or stay confused.

Why it is hard

Spoiler safety can’t be a polite instruction to the model.

You paused here · P
SeenEvery fact revealed before P
Not yet seenMust be invisible to the model, not just “not mentioned”

Models remember plots

Popular films are in the training data. Telling the model “don’t spoil” leaves the knowledge in place.

Search ranks the future too

Retrieval over a whole film puts later scenes in the candidate list. One missed filter and they reach the prompt.

Leaks look like good answers

A spoiled answer reads fluently and confidently. Nothing in the output signals that something went wrong.

✓So the model must never see what the viewer hasn’t seen. The gate has to be structural.

The spoiler gate

Gate first, then rank. The model can’t leak what it was never shown.

How eligibility works

  • ✓
    P is locked on the server. The browser reports a position; it never sends, or receives, the list of what is visible.
  • ✓
    Every row has a reveal time. It becomes eligible when reveal_at_s + margin ≤ P, with margins per type: dialogue 1 s, events 3 s, scenes 3–5 s.
  • ✓
    Names wait for the film. A character is “the young woman” until the film says her name; only then may the name be used.
  • ✓
    Identities stay apart until a reveal. Facts are never linked across a hidden identity; the clue to it is a future canary.
  • ✓
    Ranking runs inside the gate. Vector, full-text or hybrid ranking only ever sees the eligible set.

Rejected on purpose

  • Managed RAG with metadata filters

    Ranks the whole film, then filters. If the filter silently stops applying, the result is a spoiler.

  • Grounding with online search

    Brings in synopses, reviews and wikis: exactly where spoilers live.

  • Calling Gemini from the browser

    The client would need the evidence, and a client can be modified. The gate must run behind the server’s trust boundary.

The agent

An ADK agent with five gate-aware tools, and no way to move P.

story_so_farLatest eligible recap plus the scenes and events after it. The first choice for “what did I miss?”
search_momentsEmbeds the question with gemini-embedding-2 and ranks only inside the eligible set.
recent_dialogueLines spoken before P, with who said each one when known.
on_screenWho and what is in the paused frame; a tap is matched to their boxes, and labels are decided at P.
look_closerGemini 3.8 Flash looks at the paused frame plus stills from the 30 s before it.

P lives in session state

The server writes P, title, language and tap into the ADK session with state_delta. Tools read ToolContext.state; no tool takes P as an argument.

Every step is visible

gate→tool→tool_result→answer

Streamed to the browser with Server-Sent Events.

Guard, retry, fall back

If the output guard finds a forbidden term, the draft is discarded and the question is answered again in a fresh session with a do-not-use hint. If that still fails, a fixed safe answer is returned.

Community benefit

Help that respects the story, for people who need it most.

Accessibility

Deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers

“Who said that?” is answered from speaker-attributed dialogue, without spoiling what comes next.

Language

Viewers watching in a second language

Ask in Chinese about an English film. Cross-language retrieval finds the moment, and the answer comes back in your language.

Attention

Viewers who step away

A recap that stops exactly where you are, for families, interrupted evenings and long films.

Education and libraries

A study companion for film classes and library collections that explains what students have watched without giving away the ending.

Streaming, TV and creators

A second-screen companion that keeps viewers engaged instead of searching elsewhere. Creators can open their story to questions because reveal times are data they can review.

Feasibility and scalability

Built, deployed and cheap to run, with a clear path to scale.

Feasible today

  • Live on Cloud Run with three films, a public link and an open-source codebase.
  • Ingest once per film. Cached, budgeted stages; about $8 of Gemini for all three films, re-runs included.
  • Byte-reproducible packs: identical content re-exports to identical bytes and the same version.
  • Guarded spend: at most 2 instances, per-IP and daily question limits.

Ready to scale

  • Cloud Run Jobs ingest one film per task, in parallel.
  • The knowledge pack is versioned SQLite. A device form (reviewed rows only, no review log) is already defined, designed for answering on a TV or phone.
  • Prompts in Secret Manager change without rebuilding images.
  • Human review is specified: review patches, roles and a rule that names, identities and spoiler-critical items need a human.

Honest limits: the demo serves automatically pre-checked rows; production would add the human review step for names and twists. Shot captions are about 85% fully correct, and the output guards are a safety net, not a guarantee.

Roadmap

From three open films to any catalogue, on any screen.
